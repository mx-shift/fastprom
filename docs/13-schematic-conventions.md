# Schematic conventions

How the two stack sheets are drawn. Both are generated by
`tools/gen_wired.py`, with symbols from `tools/schlib.py`, and graded by
`tools/grade_schematic.py`, which enforces the rules marked *(graded)*. A
change to a sheet is checked by grading it and by rendering it and looking
at it, never by the grade alone.

The conventions follow how comparable open designs are drawn: One ROM,
PicoROM, EPROM-EMU-NG, Glasgow, the Raspberry Pi CM4 IO board, PiStorm and
TI's SN74AXC8T245EVM. Where this project departs from them, it says so
below.

## Sheet

- A3, filled. All text at KiCad's default 1.27 mm, legible on screen and on
  paper.
- Signal flow runs left to right: the source connector on the left, the
  logic in the middle, the destination connector on the right.
- The title block is filled in: title, revision (from `hw/REVISION`),
  company, and the copyright and licence notice (`tools/notice.py`).
  Nothing is drawn over it *(graded)*.

## Blocks

- Each functional group sits in a captioned rectangle: for example, the
  host socket, address and enable, data, the stack connector, the supply.
  Groups that do different jobs are visually separate. For example, the
  address and enable buffers sit apart from the data buffer.
- Each block is a rectangle with its caption in the top band. Wires may
  cross a block's edge; a block's edge never cuts a symbol, table, text or
  pin, blocks never touch or overlap (1.27 mm apart at least) or reach the
  title block, and every part and table sits wholly inside one *(graded:
  `block_edges`, `block_overlaps`, `uncaptioned_blocks`, `unframed`)*.
  `Sheet.group` and `Sheet.frames()` in `tools/schlib.py` draw them.
- **Power is its own block**, in a corner or along the top, apart from the
  logic. Regulators, input OR-ing and protection, and bulk capacitors go
  there; the logic reaches them only through rail symbols. A supply is one
  or the other, never both: if it gets a rail symbol anywhere (USB VBUS,
  `+5V_USB`, at J4's pads, the ESD clamp and the OR-ing diode), it gets one
  everywhere, with no net label or long wire carrying it between blocks.
- **Decoupling** for a chip is a captioned row next to it: the rail symbol at
  the row's left end, the capacitors hanging down to ground. A single
  capacitor is no row: it is one vertical line, the rail symbol above it
  and ground below. An oscillator's capacitor stays inside the
  oscillator's own block.

## Connectors

- Drawn in their physical pin order: a DIP as a DIP (pins 1-10 down the
  left, 20-11 down the right), a DF40 as its pad rows, each as seen from the
  top of its own board. Pins are never reordered to make the wiring tidy.
- Connector signal pins connect through net-label stubs long enough to
  leave a clear gap, about 5 mm, between each label and the rail ties at
  the pins (20.32 mm).
  Label-only connections are allowed at connector pins and nowhere else
  *(graded: `label_joins`, `dangling_labels`)*. Where a connector's
  signals can be drawn to what they serve without a tangle, they are drawn
  wires instead: J4's D+/D- from the ESD
  clamp (the flipped pair tied on by short verticals) and its CC pads to
  their pull-downs.
- A connector's rail pins are not stubbed. Each rail's pins are tied by a
  vertical wire close to the pins, one tie per rail (ground 2.54 mm out,
  a supply 5.08 mm, the next 7.62 mm), ending in that rail's one upright
  symbol: ground below the tie, a supply above it, never a symbol per pin
  and never a route over or around a symbol. Signal stubs leaving the
  connector, and the wires of an interleaved rail's pins, simply cross the
  ties, with no dot. That crossing is the convention at a connector, so
  the grader does not count it; every other crossing counts *(graded:
  `crossings`)*.

## Symbols

- One library symbol per part, used unchanged by every instance. For
  example, U2, U3 and U4 are all the same `SN74LVT245B`: A1-A8 down the
  left matched row for row with B1-B8 on the right, and DIR and ~OE grouped
  on the left below the A pins. Where a pin's role differs between
  instances, use KiCad's alternate pin functions rather than a second
  symbol.
- Every part is drawn as what it is: a resistor as a box, a capacitor as two
  plates, a Schottky diode as a triangle and hooked bar with the cathode on
  pin 1's side, a ferrite bead as an angled box (`two_pin()` in
  `tools/schlib.py` picks the graphic from the reference prefix). A
  resistor network is drawn as resistors in a package outline, each element
  between its two pins.
- Two-pin parts (resistors, capacitors, diodes, beads) show no pin
  numbers: their ends are interchangeable, or the graphic shows the
  cathode. Resistor networks and ICs keep theirs.

- A part's reference and value read as its own: a vertical passive has
  both stacked to its right, reference over value, and every part's text
  sits at most half as far from it as from any other part *(graded:
  `text_ownership`)*. Rows of parts are spaced to keep it so (decoupling
  rows at 12.7 mm), and where two parts sit close, as D2 and D3 do, each
  one's text goes on its outer side.
- Parts that no wire joins keep 3.81 mm (a pitch and a half) apart, each
  taken with its reference and value, a rail symbol with its name, so
  nothing reads as belonging to a neighbour it is not connected to; and
  a part's text, or a rail symbol and its name, stays a pitch from any
  wire not on its own net *(graded: `crowded`)*.

## Wiring and nets

- **A label that joins sits on a stub.** Where a net is joined by labels,
  every one of its labels sits on the free end of a stub, a wire end that
  touches nothing else, so it reads as "this continues elsewhere"
  *(graded: `label_off_stub`)*. Parts may be drawn on the net between the
  pin and the stub's end (the socket pulls on their lines, R11 on BUF_D2,
  R21 on D_BUF_NOE), but a label partway along a wire only names it and
  hides the connection. J3's TX, RX, BOOT0 and NRST run from the AG32
  through the image select and reset blocks to stub ends, labelled, as
  J3's own stubs are: its pins come in the clip's order.
- Labels join a connector to the parts it serves, not two parts to each
  other *(graded: `label_joins`)*.
- Every other connection is a drawn wire, and every T junction has a dot
  *(graded)*.
- Pins meet through a visible piece of wire, never pin on pin: a rail
  symbol on a part's pin stands a pitch off on a short lead, and two parts
  in series have a wire between them *(graded: `pins_on_pins`)*.
- Parallel lines stay a group, level and a pitch apart. Pulls on them sit
  outside the group: pull-ups in a row above it, pull-downs in a row
  below, each on a vertical from its own line (a dot at the tap) that
  simply crosses the lines between, one column per pull so no tap is a
  four-way. The socket lines 15-19 and R12-R16, R20 are drawn this way.
- No buses. Banks of parallel signals use labels at the connectors and
  wires everywhere else.
- A rail symbol ends the net it names: it ends one wire, or sits on a pin
  with no wire, never partway along a wire or on a junction *(graded:
  `rail_mid_net`)*.
- Rail symbols are always upright: a supply's arrow points up from the top
  end of its wire, ground's bars hang down from the bottom end. Never
  sideways, not even between two connector contacts; there the contacts are
  tied into one symbol per rail, as above *(graded: `rail_sideways`)*.
- Nets are named by function, from `tools/partdb.yaml`: socket nets after
  the first part the board supports (the 512x8), and the buffered 3.3 V
  side with a `BUF_` prefix.

## Notes and tables

- The sheet does not explain what a pull-up, pull-down or terminator does.
  The reasoning lives in `DESIGN_NOTES.md`.
- Build options and the differences between supported parts are KiCad
  tables, each with its caption. A table lists only the rows that change:
  what to fit for each part (R16 or R20), and the pins whose signal differs
  between the 512x8 and the 256x8. Tables are generated from partdb, not
  typed *(graded: a table is a body nothing may run over)*.

## Reviewing a change

1. `make kicad`, then `python3 tools/grade_schematic.py
   hw/fastprom-<board>.kicad_sch --where`: no hard defects.
2. `make erc` (0 errors, 0 warnings) and `make grade` (diff_netlist PASS):
   a drawing change must not change connectivity.
3. Export the sheet to PDF and render it, then look at the whole sheet and
   at the area that changed.
